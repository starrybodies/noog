Privacy policy · NOOG 0.10.0 · October 14, 2026

What NOOG processes

On eligible pages, NOOG reads visible media, nearby captions, creator identifiers and page addresses to apply your filters. Images and sampled video frames are checked by a model bundled with the extension. Captions use local phrase rules. Content is not sent to NOOG, an AI provider or an analytics service.

Network requests

NOOG may fetch a displayed image from its original public HTTP(S) image host so the local worker can check it. Requests omit cookies and the referring page, reject redirects, and limit image size. The image host still receives the request and your IP address. Model code, weights, replacement photos and fonts are bundled; no model download is required.

Private pages

Known mail, document, chat and workspace apps, supported private-message routes, forms and editable content are excluded before analysis. NOOG cannot identify every private website. Pause unfamiliar private sites from the toolbar or restrict site access using Chrome’s extension settings. Embedded frames are skipped.

Stored locally

Filter preferences, keywords, domain pauses and creator exceptions stay in Chrome’s local extension storage. Kept-post corrections store up to 200 SHA-256 fingerprints, not the original caption or media. Image results are cached in memory for up to five minutes, up to 128 entries. Temporary model-readiness metrics contain no page content. Removing NOOG clears its extension storage; local settings can be exported or replaced through the toolbar.

Permissions

Website access enables automatic content filtering and image retrieval. Storage saves your preferences. Offscreen runs the local model in a worker. Active tab lets the toolbar show page status and pause the current site. NOOG does not collect browsing history, credentials, payment information or analytics.

Limitations and support

NOOG is experimental. It can miss unwanted material or cover ordinary content. Strict modes are optional and swaps remain reversible. Report problems at GitHub issues. Avoid including private content in public reports.
